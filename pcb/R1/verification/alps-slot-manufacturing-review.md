# Alps mounting slots against proposed AISLER process

22 October 2026. Proposed target: AISLER two-layer, 1.6 mm, 35 µm, ENIG. The [official rules](https://community.aisler.net/t/2-layer-1-6mm-35-m-enig-design-rules/3732), last updated 7 July 2025 and freshly read during this audit, require 0.30 mm PTH/slot annular ring and accept plated-slot tool diameters 0.7–2.6 mm. Other final-board rules include 0.30 mm drill spacing and copper-to-edge clearance, 0.15 mm silk stroke, 0.8 mm silk text, 0.10 mm minimum mask dam where retained, suggested 0.05 mm mask expansion, and no tented vias.

**RV1 annular-ring and slot-tool geometry: PASS. Standard-footprint mechanical acceptance: CONDITIONAL PASS, with an ordinary incoming insertion/retention check. No demonstrated interference or critical fit blocker was found.**

The actual native RV1 pads have 4×3 mm rounded-rectangle copper, corner radius 0.75 mm, enclosing a 2.1×1.8 mm oval plated hole. The shortest copper ring is 0.60 mm at the broad top/bottom of the slot; the horizontal extremity has 0.95 mm. The rounded copper corners do not reduce the minimum below 0.60 mm. `check_alps_slot.py` reads these dimensions from the native board, validates the rounded boundary, and records `alps-slot-results.json`. The three electrical Ø1 mm holes in 1.8 mm pads have 0.40 mm minimum rings. All exceed 0.30 mm. The slot uses a nominal 1.8 mm tool and 0.3 mm centerline travel, within the stated plated-slot capability.

The manufacturer's Drawing 3 recommends openings with 2.1×1.8 mm overall dimensions, but does not dimension the corner radius or cross-section of the snap-in board locks. The standard KiCad footprint explicitly supports this exact model and matches the dimensions and centers. That is useful engineering evidence. A rectangular representation of a recommended opening is not proof that sharp milled corners are necessary, nor that an oval hole interferes with the actual thin formed tabs.

A precise centered-rectangle fit criterion for the nominal horizontal capsule is:

`max(w/2 − 0.15, 0)^2 + (t/2)^2 <= 0.90^2`

where w and t are the maximum installed tab cross-section dimensions parallel to the 2.1 mm and 1.8 mm slot axes. Examples only: a 2.0×0.5 mm rectangle fits the nominal profile, as does 1.8×0.8 mm; a full 2.1×1.8 mm rectangle does not. These examples are not claims about the Alps tab dimensions. A real fit calculation must also include hole tolerance, tab tolerance, forming/retention interference and actual tab placement; the catalog's positive hole-size tolerances must not be confused with an unspecified PCB process tolerance.

The remaining uncertainty is localized to the mechanical retaining tabs. It does not affect the three verified electrical contacts, the pot resistance, the pin numbering, or the routing envelope. The installed standard footprint explicitly lists suffix 1130A5R, matches the manufacturer contact/tab centers and both nominal opening dimensions, and satisfies the actual PCB process limits. On that positive evidence, its use is accepted as normal engineering practice. The absent tab cross-section alone does not establish a plausible geometric collision beyond ordinary component-fit uncertainty, and is not a prerequisite for a pre-fabrication sample. Retain an incoming insertion/retention check; re-open this issue if actual tab geometry or fit evidence contradicts the accepted pattern. Final exported slots must still reproduce the accepted shape, size and location. There is no blanket sample prerequisite for the other verified parts, and no requirement for impossible sharp-corner milling. Do not silently enlarge the holes: excessive clearance could remove the snap-in retention function.
